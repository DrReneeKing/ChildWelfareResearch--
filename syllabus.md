## Course Description

CUSW 403, Practice Competency I, introduces generalist social work practice through the Generalist Intervention Model (GIM). This pilot section incorporates a Pre-Practice Experience (PPE): students build and benchmark engagement, assessment, and planning skills through simulation and a shared case study before rotating through supervised observation in child welfare, mental health, and community-organization settings. The course also introduces students to the ethical use of AI tools in generalist practice. The Afrocentric Perspective — the School's culturally grounded practice framework — is applied throughout, with specific focus in this course on Matrix Roles, Intuitive, and Humanistic Values.

## Course Learning Objectives

Upon completion of the course, students will be able to:

1. Explain core social work knowledge, skills, and professional behaviors for generalist social work practice. *(Module 1–2)*
2. Describe the services, programs, and interventions for various social problems. *(Modules 5–7)*
3. Identify and discuss the social themes of social justice, cultural competence, intersectionality, and spirituality that impact people's lives in their social environments. *(Modules 6–7)*
4. Identify the sources of knowledge for the social work profession. *(Module 1; reinforced concurrently in CUSW 401, 411, 333)*
5. Identify the function and purpose of professional social work organizations. *(Module 2)*
6. Identify the role of professional social workers with diverse, special, and at-risk populations. *(Modules 5–7)*

## Grading Policy

A grade of **C or better is required** to pass this course. Grade ranges: A (90–100), B (80–89), C (70–79), D (60–69), F (below 60).

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|
| Attendance & Participation | 10% |
| Orientation Quiz (Module 1) | 5% |
| AI Ethics Reflection (Module 2) | 5% |
| Engagement Simulation (Module 3) | 15% |
| First Client Case (Module 4) | 15% |
| Child Welfare Observation Log + Reflection (Module 5) | 10% |
| Afrocentric Perspective Case Study (Module 6) | 10% |
| Community Asset Map + Supervisor Mini-Evaluation (Module 7) | 10% |
| Professional Portfolio + Field Readiness Evaluation (Module 8) | 10% |
| Final Exam | 10% |

**Phase 1 Benchmark Checkpoint:** the Module 4 checkpoint requires a minimum 80% to advance to Phase 2 agency rotations — this is a readiness gate in addition to its 15% weight above, not simply part of the running average. A student below 80% receives a 1-week remediation window before starting rotations (Phase 2 begins Week 9 instead of Week 8 for that student).

**Field Readiness Evaluation (Module 8):** independent of the numeric course grade above. Rated **Ready / Ready with Conditions / Not Ready**:
- *Ready with Conditions* does not block progression — a documented Field Readiness Conditions Memo is handed off to the CUSW 404 instructor.
- *Not Ready* blocks CUSW 404 enrollment (not automatically a retake) and triggers a **PPE Development and Improvement Plan** — a time-bound remediation plan co-reviewed by the instructor and a second faculty reader or the BSW Program Director. If not completed successfully by the review date, the defined fallback is retaking CUSW 403.

## Academic Integrity / AI Use Policy

This course explicitly teaches AI tools as part of generalist practice (Module 2) and provides two course-specific assistants: the **CUSW 403 Practice Coach** (see `resources/course-ai-assistant.md`), which coaches on the Engagement Simulation, First Client Case reflections, and Afrocentric Perspective Case Study without ever drafting submittable text; and the **CUSW 403 Course TA** (see `resources/course-ta-assistant.md`), which answers general logistics, policy, and concept questions directly. Appropriate use:
- The Practice Coach (and any other AI tool used in this course) may be used for feedback, self-assessment, and coaching questions — never to draft text a student submits as their own work.
- The Course TA answers factual and logistics questions directly, but does not draft graded work either — that request routes to the Practice Coach instead.
- AI tools may **not** be used to generate graded reflections, case analyses, or portfolio content submitted as the student's own work.
- Client-identifying information — real or from a rotation site — must never be entered into either assistant, or any AI tool. All case materials used with AI tools must be de-identified and composite, per the confidentiality standard applied throughout the course (see `schedule.md`, Module 6 note).
