## Course Description

This course provides students with knowledge and information regarding how to assess and identify cases of child abuse and neglect. Students will gain general knowledge and understanding of the types of abuse and neglect, methods of investigations, interviewing techniques, as well as knowledge of available community resources to both prevent and treat child abuse.

**Co-Requisite:** CUSW 402, CUSW 404, and CUSW 416.

## Course Objectives

- Discuss maltreatment of children from a historical perspective
- Explain the types of intervention strategies in child abuse and neglect cases
- Explain the legal response to child abuse and neglect
- Identify signs and symptoms of child abuse and neglect cases
- Define, discuss, and distinguish the 4 different types of child maltreatment
- Discuss the dynamics for working in child protection services

## Learning Outcomes
_(pulled from `course-toolkit/learning-outcomes-map.md`; public-safe summary — the DSSWE data-science layer integrated across all 8 modules per the AUC Data Science and Analytics Framework)_

Students will, in addition to the content objectives above: define data science and its child-welfare applications; identify and evaluate child welfare data sources (AFCARS, NCANDS); use RStudio/Posit and R (tidyverse) to wrangle and analyze child welfare data; build data visualizations (ggplot2) of child welfare trends; and apply ethical, bias-aware reasoning (NASW Code of Ethics) to data-driven child welfare practice — culminating in an independent capstone data-science project using real AFCARS foster care data. Full outcome-by-outcome mapping (assessment, AUC Framework area, EPAS competency) lives in `course-toolkit/learning-outcomes-map.md`.

## Academic Integrity / AI Use Policy

All acts of academic dishonesty (cheating, plagiarism, having another person write one's work, fabricating research data, false excuses) violate social work values/ethics and University policy, and are subject to the accusation/penalty/appeal procedures in the BSW Student Handbook 2021. Students who submit work that is not their own forfeit the opportunity to continue at Clark Atlanta University.

**Artificial Intelligence (AI) Use:** The course-approved AI tool is **[Using R for Child Welfare Research](https://chatgpt.com/g/g-6756c658ff388191bffe594efd98e161-using-r-for-child-welfare-research)**, a custom GPT built for this course. Students are encouraged to use it — or other AI tools, with instructor approval — as a learning support tool, not as a substitute for their own thinking, analysis, or writing. Any use of AI must be disclosed through an AI Transparency Statement (see the Ethical and Responsible Use of AI assignment, Module 03). Students remain responsible for verifying the accuracy of AI-generated content, citing appropriate sources, protecting confidential information, and ensuring that all submitted work reflects their own critical thinking and professional judgment. Undisclosed AI use, fabrication of sources, or submission of AI-generated work as one's own may constitute a violation of the institution's academic integrity policy.
