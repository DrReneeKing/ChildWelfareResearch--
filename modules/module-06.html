Trauma-Informed Practice, Legal Response & Data Science Resumes

Overview

The data-science thread resumes here after a two-module pause, running in parallel with content on trauma-informed practice and the legal system's response to child abuse and neglect.

Learning Outcome

Apply trauma-informed principles to child welfare practice; explain the legal response to child abuse and neglect and the rights and responsibilities of families within it; identify sources and types of data relevant to child welfare research.

Weekly Breakdown

Week 13 (10/28/26, asynchronous) — DSSWE Module 1 Continued ▸
  • One Pager: Introduction to Data Science (review/deepen Week 7's framework introduction)
  • Data Science Activity #1: identifying a research topic, research questions, and variables — the first concrete step toward the semester capstone project
Week 14 (10/30/26) — Trauma-Informed Approaches & Legal Response ▸
  • Trauma-informed approaches to competent practice: recognizing trauma responses, avoiding re-traumatization
  • The legal response to child abuse and neglect: how the legal system intersects with child protective services
  • The family: roles, responsibilities, and rights within legal proceedings
  • DS Touchpoint (Week 14): map one variable from your Data Science Activity #1 research topic to an actual NCANDS or AFCARS field. Maps to the Pre/Post-Survey's data-source-application item — applying Week 2's awareness, not just recalling it.
  • Revisiting Kenny A. v. Perdue: now that students have applied foster-care placement and disposition reasoning in the Case Study (Module 04), return to the consent decree first introduced in Module 02 in more depth — specific practice standards (caseload limits, sibling co-placement, timely health screenings, reduced congregate care), the role of the independent accountability agents and their semi-annual public reports, and the exit-plan mechanism (three consecutive compliant six-month periods). Have students re-examine their own Case Study foster-care/disposition answers against these actual standards: did their placement recommendation satisfy sibling co-placement? Would their timeline for a health screening have met the consent decree's requirement?
Week 15 (11/4/26, asynchronous) — DSSWE Module 2: Sources and Types of Data ▸
  • Sources of data relevant to child welfare (AFCARS, NCANDS, agency administrative data)
  • Types of data: quantitative vs. qualitative, structured vs. unstructured
  • Certificate: Data Science Methodology

This is where the capstone project actually starts taking shape: Data Science Activity #1 has students commit to a real research topic and identify the variables they'll need. DSSWE Module 2 then supplies the "where does data come from" foundation — AFCARS and NCANDS, the same restricted-use datasets students will work with directly later. Still no coding yet; this module is entirely about framing the right question before touching data.
