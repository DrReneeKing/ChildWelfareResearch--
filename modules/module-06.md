## Week 10 — Afrocentric Perspective Foundations; Rotation Begins
- The Afrocentric Perspective: definition, historical origin (Atlanta School of Social Work, 1920 → Clark Atlanta University, 1988), and its 10 concepts overall
- This course's three assigned concepts, in depth:
  - **Humanistic Values** — values that place priority on eliminating human oppression and enhancing human potential; valuing human values over all values
  - **Intuitive** — recognizing and accepting the importance of emotion in determining what is real
  - **Matrix Roles** — personification of effective professional strategies for eliminating human oppression and enhancing human potential
- Mental health rotation begins: 4 hrs/week at assigned mental health agency site

## Week 11 — The DIASPORA Model
- Introducing the DIASPORA Model (King, 2019) as a structured framework for analyzing a social issue through the Afrocentric Perspective: **D**econstruct → **I**nvestigate → **A**cknowledge/Assess → **S**ocial structures → **P**olitical objectives → **O**pportunities/Oppression → **R**econstruct → **A**frocentricity/Affective Knowledge
- Applying the model to a de-identified, composite case drawn from the rotation experience
- **Due:** Afrocentric Perspective Case Study (small-group) + Flipgrid video

## Assignment: Afrocentric Perspective Case Study

Small groups apply the DIASPORA Model to a de-identified, composite case drawn loosely from members' Mental Health rotation experience — **no real client names, identifying details, or agency-identifying information**, consistent with the confidentiality standard used throughout this course. Groups work through each DIASPORA step, then present findings via a Flipgrid video.
