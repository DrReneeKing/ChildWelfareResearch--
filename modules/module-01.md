# Module 01: Introduction, Course Overview & Mandated Reporting

**Weeks:** 1–2 (Wed 8/19/26 & Wed 8/26/26)

## Overview

This orientation module establishes the foundation for the semester: course structure, expectations, and technology tools, followed by the legal and ethical starting point for all child welfare practice — mandated reporting. It also plants the first seed of the course's data-science thread: by Week 1's end, students should understand that this course pairs child welfare content with hands-on data science, using real federal datasets (AFCARS, NCANDS) to ground policy discussions in actual numbers — and that no prior coding experience is expected or required.

## Learning Outcome

Discuss maltreatment of children from a historical perspective and describe the dynamics and legal obligations of working in child protection services, including mandated reporting requirements.

## Week 1 (8/19/26) — Course Orientation

- Syllabus walkthrough: course objectives, grading structure, policies (academic integrity, disability accommodations, attendance)
- Technology and tools overview: Canvas navigation, Zoom expectations, Yellowdig Learning Community setup, and the course-approved AI tool — [Using R for Child Welfare Research](https://chatgpt.com/g/g-6756c658ff388191bffe594efd98e161-using-r-for-child-welfare-research) (a custom GPT students will use starting in Module 03 — nothing to do with it yet, just know it exists)
- Introduction to the child welfare field: scope, historical context, key terminology students will use all semester
- First-day housekeeping: Roll Call Attendance, Turnitin setup, Netiquette agreement, Withdrawal Policy acknowledgment
- **DS Touchpoint (Week 1):** create a free RStudio/Posit Cloud account — setup only, no coding. *Maps to the Pre/Post-Survey's RStudio-environment-familiarity item, establishing the baseline before any instruction happens.* Bookmark the [R & RStudio Reference Guide](../resources/r-rstudio-reference-guide.html) now — it's the standing resource every DS Touchpoint points back to all semester.

## Week 2 (8/26/26) — Mandated Reporting

- Legal definition of a mandated reporter and who qualifies under Georgia law
- The reporting process: what triggers a report, how and to whom it's made, reasonable-cause standard
- Consequences of failure to report; legal protections for reporters acting in good faith
- Connecting mandated reporting to the semester's larger arc: this is the entry point into the formal child protection system that later modules (assessment, intervention) build on
- **DS Touchpoint (Week 2) — Child Neglect Data Activity:** students visit the [Child Maltreatment Report](https://acf.gov/cb/data-research/child-maltreatment) (the annual NCANDS-sourced federal report), download the most recent edition, and search it for their home state's neglect victim count/rate, noting the table/page number and writing a 2–3 sentence reflection on what the number suggests about child welfare needs in that state. Optional extension: compare against the state's own Department of Family and Children Services data. This replaces a passive "here's what NCANDS is" tour with hands-on practice reading a real federal report — still zero coding. *Maps to the Pre/Post-Survey's data-source-awareness item.*
- **AI touchpoint (Week 2):** students do a short mandated-reporter role-play with the course-approved AI tool — describing a hypothetical concerning situation and having the tool walk them through the reasonable-cause standard and reporting process — as a low-stakes way to rehearse the Week 2 content before the Mandated Reporter Certificate assessment.

## Data Science Tie-In

Formal data-science activities (DSSWE Module 1) don't begin until the Week 4–5 asynchronous window (see `schedule.md`), but Module 01 is where students get prepared for a data-science-integrated course — and where the first concrete commitment gets named, so it isn't a surprise later:

- **Name it early.** Tell students directly, in Week 1, that this course integrates data science throughout the semester — not as an add-on, but as a way of grounding child welfare policy and practice in real evidence.
- **Address apprehension up front.** Prior DSSWE cohorts reported feeling intimidated by the coding component when it arrived without warning. Framing it early — no prior coding experience needed, skills build incrementally module by module — measurably reduces that anxiety.
- **The Pre-Survey is the floor, not the ceiling.** At minimum, every student completes the DSSWE Pre-Survey (~15 minutes, required for participation, ungraded) by 9/11/26 — before any coding begins. Even a student who engages with nothing else data-science-related this module has still taken this first step.
- **Low-stakes setup, Week 1:** have students confirm access to the required *R for Data Science* text and create a free RStudio/Posit Cloud account. No coding yet — just making sure the tools are in place before they're needed.
- **First real data, Week 2:** the Child Neglect Data Activity puts students inside an actual NCANDS-sourced federal report before they've written a line of code — pulling their own home state's neglect number gives the semester's recurring "neglect = largest share of substantiated maltreatment" framing a personal anchor point from day one, and previews the exact kind of state-level lookup the Case Study's disproportionality data will build on later.
