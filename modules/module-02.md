## Optional: Where AI Bias Actually Comes From

AI/ML models like the ones discussed in this module are built by training on large datasets, then sometimes further "fine-tuned" on more specific data. Bias enters at both stages: if the training data underrepresents or stereotypes a population, the model's outputs will reflect that, often invisibly. This is a technical process, not a moral failing baked into the technology — but it's exactly why the confidentiality and bias concerns this module raises are grounded in something real about how these tools are actually built, not just a general caution. *(Not required reading, and not part of the AI Ethics Reflection grade — included for students who want the technical picture behind the ethics.)*

## Assignment: AI Ethics Reflection

An individual written reflection responding to prompts about the ethical tensions AI tools raise in social work practice — confidentiality, bias, client trust, appropriate boundaries. Students may use the Practice Coach to think through the prompt (it will not draft their answer for them — see `resources/course-ai-assistant.md`). This assignment is itself a live example of the ethical boundary it asks students to think about.
