## Purpose

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools are becoming common in education, research, and professional social work practice. While AI can improve efficiency and support learning, it also presents ethical challenges related to accuracy, privacy, bias, transparency, authorship, and professional responsibility.

This assignment will help you develop the skills needed to use AI responsibly while maintaining academic integrity and the ethical standards of the social work profession.

## Learning Objectives

After completing this assignment, students will be able to:

- Explain ethical benefits and risks associated with AI.
- Apply the NASW Code of Ethics when using AI.
- Critically evaluate AI-generated information.
- Recognize bias and limitations in AI outputs.
- Practice transparency by documenting AI use.
- Reflect on responsible AI use in research and professional practice.

## Part 2: Evaluate the AI Response

Answer the following questions.

**Accuracy**
- Was the information accurate?
- What facts required verification?
- What errors did you identify?

**Bias**
- Did the AI appear biased?
- Were important perspectives missing?
- Were vulnerable populations represented fairly?
- Given the level (micro/mezzo/macro) you identified in Part 1: whose voice is easiest for an AI tool to leave out at that level? (A micro-level case description that omits the family's own account; a mezzo-level agency summary that omits frontline caseworker perspective; a macro-level policy explanation that omits the population most affected by it.)

**A required question regardless of your Part 1 topic:** Predictive risk-assessment tools (e.g., algorithmic hotline-screening tools some child welfare agencies use to help decide which reports to investigate) are an active, real controversy in the field — Module 09 revisits this at the capstone. In 2–3 sentences, explain one bias concern such a tool could raise, drawing on the disproportionality data from Modules 04–05 (Georgia's substantiated-victim population is roughly 42% Black against a Black population share of roughly a third statewide).

**Ethics** — discuss each of the following:
- Confidentiality
- Privacy
- Informed consent
- Cultural humility
- Algorithmic bias
- Professional responsibility

**Academic Integrity**
- How should AI be acknowledged?
- When does AI assistance become plagiarism?
- How would you cite AI?

**Human Judgment**
- What parts of the assignment should never be delegated entirely to AI? Examples: ethical decision making, client assessment, interpreting findings, making recommendations.
